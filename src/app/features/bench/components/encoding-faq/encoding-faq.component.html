What changes in the file, and what you see on screen.

Will this add detail to my image?

No. It increases the brightness of existing pixels. It does not sharpen, reconstruct textures, or recover information from blown-out highlights or crushed shadows. A larger output size does not create real detail either.

Why might the result look different on another screen?

The visible boost depends on the display’s HDR capability, available brightness, system settings, and the viewer’s HDR support. An SDR screen cannot show the same peak brightness, and the result may be tone-mapped or displayed differently.

Does more exposure always look better?

Not necessarily. Start low and increase it while checking your highlights. The encoder uses 203 nits as reference white before the boost; encoded values above PQ’s 10,000-nit limit are clipped. Those numbers describe the file, not the brightness your screen is guaranteed to produce.

Which format should I download?

Use PNG when you need transparency and enable Preserve transparency. JPEG uses the selected background instead. Both outputs carry HDR color information, but support varies between viewers. Dithering helps reduce banding in the 8-bit PQ output; it does not add image detail.

Where is my image processed?

Processing happens locally in your browser, on your device. Your image is not uploaded to a server for encoding.
